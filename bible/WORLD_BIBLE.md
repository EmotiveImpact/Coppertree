# World bible
Development status: proposed unless explicitly confirmed in the decisions register.

## Purpose
A joyful shared world centred on Black childhood: friendship, curiosity, creativity, family and everyday adventure. Children should be able to see themselves exploring, making mistakes, receiving care and contributing ideas.

## Parent-facing promise (draft)
Joyful adventures that celebrate Black childhood and invite children to explore feelings, solve problems and make their own mark.
Describe skills as encouraged and practised, not guaranteed developmental outcomes. Entertainment, warmth and humour must carry the stories.

## Emotional foundations
Belonging; confidence to try; emotional understanding; curiosity and judgement; care and responsibility. Being capable includes asking for help, resting, apologising and trying again. Each child remains valued when they struggle.

## Home
Copper Tree: a fictional London neighbourhood, visually informed by South London terraces, estates, markets, railway arches and green spaces. Named for a copper beech in the community garden. Exact borough and geography are not established.
The changing seasons alter its colours and adventures. Build an internally consistent street plan before using walking times or routes in stories.

## Tone and story rules
Warm, funny, curious and grounded. Small mysteries can feel enormous to a child. Imagination sequences may expand into space or fantasy, with visual cues distinguishing them from everyday reality. Magic is not established canon.
Families and adults are present. Trips beyond the neighbourhood involve appropriate adults. Children have distinct personalities and family histories, not one interchangeable Black experience. Heritage details remain to be developed and researched when relevant.
No child is permanently confined to their hobby. Let the quiet child lead, the sporty child create and the confident child need help.
Readers contribute choices and drawings without needing to colour in one correct way.

## Shared-universe continuity
Each book stands alone. Recurring objects, changing plants and completed inventions reward readers who recognise them. Distinguish the order books are released from the order events happen in the story.
